## VERDICT UP FRONT

**No clone. Surviving wedge — but one piece of prior art lands very close and must be cited on stage.**

- **Closest prior art:** arXiv:2605.13706, *Identifying AI Web Scrapers Using Canary Tokens* (May 2026).
  It already does **per-visitor unique canary token minting**. It does **not** do propagation
  between clients — its verification channel is an LLM's output, on a two-month lag.
- **Tarpit risk is real but it is a *framing* tarpit, not a build tarpit:** the crowded,
  well-known neighbours (Nepenthes, Pyison, Cloudflare AI Labyrinth, Anubis) all serve
  decoy/poison/PoW content to *waste* crawler resources. A judge who pattern-matches
  "serves fake pages to crawlers" will file Canary Maze next to them. The pitch must lead
  with *attribution*, not with *decoys*.
- **Mechanism wedge that survives everything I opened:** nothing I opened observes a secret
  minted for client A being re-requested by client B, or emits a propagation graph +
  offline-verifiable evidence bundle. The *inter-client* edge is unoccupied.

## 1. `startup` — commercial products

### Canarytokens / Thinkst — the incumbent primitive
- https://docs.canarytokens.org/guide/ — 2026-10 (accessed)
- Self-description, verbatim: Canarytokens function as **"motion sensors for your networks,
  computers and clouds."** They are placed so operators **"get a clear alarm if they are accessed."**
- 30+ token types: DNS, HTTP, QR code, web image, AWS/Azure/CrowdStrike creds, PDF/Word/Excel/SVG,
  Kubeconfig, SQL Server, MySQL dump, WireGuard, fake app, cloned website, IdP SAML.
- **Per-visitor minting:** the overview page I opened does not mention per-request or per-visitor
  token generation. The model it describes is one token *per protected asset*, generated by a human
  in a web form, then planted. I did not open thinkst.com or every per-token guide, so this is
  "not established on the page I opened", not a proven absence.
- **Propagation between clients:** the page I opened contains nothing about tracking a token
  across clients or fingerprints. The unit of output is an **alarm**, i.e. "token X fired."
- **Propagation graph:** nothing on the page I opened.
- **How Canary Maze differs:** Canarytokens answers *"was this asset touched?"* — one token, one
  alert, no notion of who the token was issued to. Canary Maze answers *"did these two clients
  talk?"* — which requires (a) minting a fresh token for every visitor automatically at request
  time, (b) a mint ledger binding token → fingerprint → timestamp, (c) sighting detection keyed on
  *fingerprint mismatch* rather than on the token firing at all. In Canarytokens' model every fire
  is a hit; in Canary Maze a fire by the *original* fingerprint is a no-op and only a fire by a
  *different* one is the signal. That inversion is the whole product.
- **Wedge, not clone.** Canarytokens is the correct primitive to cite as validation and the correct
  thing to say Canary Maze automates per-visitor.

### Bot-management / anti-scraping vendors
- DataDome — https://datadome.co/guides/bot-protection/bot-detection-how-to-identify-bot-traffic-to-your-website/ — 2026-10 (accessed, via search)
  and https://datadome.co/threat-research/the-art-of-bot-detection-picasso-for-device-class-fingerprinting/ (LEAD, not opened)
- Netacea, HUMAN, Fingerprint.com — covered only through the comparison search below (LEADS).
- What the scan turned up: every vendor mechanism described is **similarity-side**, i.e. exactly the
  thing the idea's insight says is failing. DataDome's stated edge is **cross-network reputation** —
  how the same fingerprint or IP behaved across DataDome's whole customer base, so a fingerprint
  that misbehaved elsewhere arrives pre-marked. Netacea is server-side-only with no client-side JS.
  Picasso is device-class fingerprinting.
- **Crucially:** cross-network reputation links clients **by resemblance and by shared IP/fingerprint
  history**, which is still a similarity judgement with classifier precision. It does not plant a
  secret. None of the vendor descriptions I read plants content in order to measure sharing between
  clients.
- **Honest limit:** I did not open HUMAN, Netacea or Fingerprint.com product pages directly. So:
  *none of the vendor descriptions I opened does planted-secret inter-client attribution* — stated
  as scan coverage, not as a fact about the market.

### Content provenance / AI-crawler audit vendors
- Partially checked. What surfaced were monitoring/reporting tools, not planting tools:
  - https://web-alert.io/blog/ai-crawler-bot-monitoring-gptbot-claudebot-perplexitybot-guide (LEAD)
  - https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/ai-crawlers (LEAD)

## 3. `research` — papers and prototypes

### ★ arXiv:2605.13706 — *Identifying AI Web Scrapers Using Canary Tokens* (THE close one)
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13706 and https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13706v1 — 2026-10 (accessed).
  Submitted 2026-05. Seiden, Ren, Zhang, Kim, Liu, Wenger (Duke).
- Verbatim from the abstract: **"We host dynamic websites that serve unique canary tokens to each
  visiting scraper."** And: **"then prompt LLMs for information about our sites. If an LLM
  consistently generates outputs containing tokens unique to a scraper, it provides evidence of
  exposure to that scraper."** Result: **"our approach can reliably identify which scrapers feed
  which LLM, including several that are not publicly known or disclosed by the companies"** across
  22 production LLM systems.
- **This paper owns the "per-visitor unique canary token" primitive.** Do not claim that as novel.
- **Where it stops, confirmed against the full HTML:**
  - Inter-client propagation: **not observed.** Tokens are minted per *scraper identity*
    (User-Agent + ASN pair), and the paper does not investigate a token minted for one client being
    re-requested by a different client/IP/fingerprint.
  - Propagation graph / timeline between web clients: **not constructed.** The analysis is
    "mapping AI chatbots to scrapers" on final chatbot outputs, not intermediate paths.
  - Timescale: verbatim — **"we wait for two months to allow the scrapers to retrieve the content
    from our websites and feed the content into AI chatbots."** Follow-ups at one- and two-week
    intervals.
  - Time-to-first-crawl: not measured. Verbatim: **"anecdotal evidence suggests websites of our
    size could take up to six weeks to propagate online."**
  - Offline-verifiable evidence bundle: no.
- **How Canary Maze differs, precisely — four axes:**
  1. **Verification channel.** Paper: the *model's output*, obtained by prompting 22 chatbots.
     Canary Maze: the *site's own request log*. No model in the loop on the verbatim path.
  2. **Latency.** Paper: two months. Canary Maze: minutes, because a sighting is an HTTP request.
  3. **Relation proven.** Paper: scraper → LLM *ingestion*. Canary Maze: client A → client B
     *information sharing*. Different edge in the graph.
  4. **Artefact.** Paper: a research table. Canary Maze: a propagation graph plus an evidence bundle
     a third party verifies offline — because the user is an operator being asked to prove something,
     not a researcher publishing.
  The paper is the **strongest intellectual prior art** and also the **best possible validation**:
  a Duke team spent two months proving the primitive works. Canary Maze moves it from
  ingestion-detection to inter-client propagation and from two months to minutes.

### Other research

  https://apartresearch.com/news/ai-hackers-in-the-wild-llm-agent-honeypot and

- **arXiv:2508.03130** *Protecting Small Organizations from AI Bots with Logrip: Hierarchical IP

## 4. `platform` — what the big platforms already ship

### Cloudflare AI Labyrinth (adjacent, and the thing judges will confuse this with)
- https://blog.cloudflare.com/ai-labyrinth/ — 2026-10 (accessed).
- Mechanism: generative-AI decoy pages, **pre-generated and stored in R2**, served when bot activity
  is detected. Not per-visitor unique.
- Verbatim: **"When bots hit these URLs, we can be confident they aren't actual humans, and this
  information is recorded and automatically fed to our machine learning models."**
  **"This provides us with a powerful identification mechanism, generating valuable data that feeds
  into our machine learning models."**
  **"By analyzing which crawlers are following these hidden pathways, we can identify new bot
  patterns."**
- **So:** the decoys are a **labelling source for a classifier**. The output is a better
  similarity model — precisely the approach the idea's insight says is failing. Cloudflare plants
  content to *identify bots*, not to *prove two clients shared information*.
- **Differs from Canary Maze:** shared decoy corpus vs. per-visitor unique secret; classifier
  training vs. a propagation fact; no mint ledger, no inter-client sighting, no graph, no
  operator-held evidence bundle. And the false-positive story is the inverse: Labyrinth's confidence
  is classifier precision, Canary Maze's is HMAC collision.
- **Fork base:** not applicable, closed source. Pyison/nepenthes-py are the open equivalents.

### Cloudflare AI Crawl Control
- https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/ — 2026-10 (accessed).
- Features, verbatim: **"See which AI services access your content"**; track **"crawler activity and
  request patterns"**; **"Set allow or block rules for individual crawlers"**; **"Track which
  crawlers follow your directives and create enforcement rules"**; pay-per-crawl monetisation (beta).
- The page I opened makes no mention of per-visitor unique content, canary tokens, propagation
  tracking between clients, or evidence bundles.
- **Differs:** this is per-crawler accounting against a *declared identity*. It answers "how much did
  GPTBot take". It cannot answer "did the thing calling itself Chrome get its URL from GPTBot".

### ★ Cloudflare vs Perplexity, Aug 2025 — the closest *methodology* precedent anywhere
- Reported 2025-08: https://tech.slashdot.org/story/25/08/04/1459240/perplexity-is-using-stealth-undeclared-crawlers-to-evade-website-no-crawl-directives-cloudflare-says
  and https://www.mi-3.com.au/05-08-2025/cloudflare-springs-trap-accuses-perplexity-stealth-scraping-scale
  (both LEADS via search; I did not open Cloudflare's own post).
- Method: Cloudflare **spun up brand-new domains**, locked them with robots.txt, blocked every
  crawler, **then asked Perplexity to summarise those sites** — and Perplexity returned content it
  could only have fetched. Signals: undeclared IPs outside the published range, rotating IPs and
  ASNs, user-agent disguised as Chrome on macOS. Scale: declared crawlers 20–25M req/day, undeclared
  3–6M req/day. Cloudflare removed Perplexity from its verified-bot programme. ChatGPT, tested the
  same way, stopped when disallowed.
- **This is a create+verify attribution experiment run by the biggest platform, on new domains,
  and it worked.** It is the single best evidence that the idea's premise is real and that the
  demo is achievable.
- **How Canary Maze differs:** Cloudflare's trap was **manual, one-off, and verified through a
  chatbot answer** (same channel as the Duke paper — ask the model). It proved *an operator fetched
  content it shouldn't have*. Canary Maze is **automated, per-visitor, continuous**, verified in the
  **request log**, and proves the *different* proposition that **two clients shared a secret**, with
  a timeline and a portable evidence bundle. Cloudflare also did not publish the raw request records
  as an offline-verifiable bundle — the industry had to take Cloudflare's word for it, and Perplexity
  publicly disputed it. **That dispute is the product.** The evidence bundle exists precisely because
  "trust the big vendor's dashboard" failed in the most prominent case to date.

### Cloudflare Web Bot Auth / signed agents
- https://blog.cloudflare.com/signed-agents/ , https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/reference/bot-verification/web-bot-auth
  — 2026-10 (accessed via search).
- HTTP Message Signatures (RFC 9421), Ed25519; the agent signs the target origin, verified against a
  published key directory. Cloudflare + Anthropic + OpenAI in production; Claude, ChatGPT,
  Perplexity, Common Crawl listed.
- Noted limitation, verbatim from the coverage: **"Verification proves operator identity only. It is
  not authorization, not intent, and not a decision about whether the request is welcome."**
- **This is Canary Maze's best friend, not a competitor, and it is a complement to pitch explicitly.**
  Web Bot Auth tells you *who a cooperating client claims to be*. It says nothing about
  non-signing clients, and nothing about whether two clients shared data. Canary Maze covers exactly
  the gap Cloudflare itself names: it is about **what happened between clients**, which signatures
  cannot express. A signed request that fetches a token minted for a *different* fingerprint is the
  highest-quality evidence either system can produce — signature gives you the operator name, the
  mint ledger gives you the leak. Build the demo to show that pairing.

## 8. ★ CAN YOU GET REAL AGENT TRAFFIC TO A BRAND-NEW SITE IN ~24 HOURS?

**Yes. A live demo is realistic, and the fast path is well documented. Seed a simulated run as
insurance, but plan for live.**

### Evidence that crawlers arrive at new domains unprompted, within minutes
- https://metehanai.substack.com/p/i-built-a-60000-page-ai-website-for — 2026-10 (accessed).
  Brand-new domain, zero authority, zero backlinks. Verbatim: **"Within minutes of deploying, GPTBot
  started crawling."** Discovery mechanism, verbatim: **"It found the site through the XML sitemap
  and just started consuming everything."** No submission or IndexNow ping mentioned.
  **First 12 hours:** GPTBot 29,000+ requests; GoogleOther 60+; Googlebot 11; AhrefsBot 7;
  PerplexityBot 2; **ChatGPT-User 1**; OAI-SearchBot present. Verbatim: **"GPTBot made 5,200+
  requests while Googlebot made just 11 — a 470x difference."**
- https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/agentic-crawler-behavior-30-day-site-log-study (LEAD):
  on a fresh zero-authority domain GPTBot made **470x more requests than Googlebot in the first
  24 hours**; 30-day medians across 12 sites — GPTBot ~4,200 hits/day (breadth-first),
  ClaudeBot ~1,800 (depth-first), PerplexityBot ~980. Notable: **PerplexityBot shows almost no
  scheduled background crawl and fetches a site only when a user query references the domain.**
- Cloudflare's Perplexity experiment (above) was itself run on **freshly spun-up domains** — the
  platform-scale proof that this works on a new domain.

### The demo this actually buys you — and it is better than "lots of crawlers"
The propagation demo needs a *second* fingerprint fetching a token minted for a *first*. Three live
paths, in descending reliability:

1. **Crawler → user-agent handoff, same operator (highest value, nearly free).**
   `GPTBot` crawls and gets token T minted against its fingerprint. Later `ChatGPT-User` or
   `OAI-SearchBot` — a *different* fingerprint, different IP range, different declared purpose —
   fetches a URL containing T. That is a textbook sighting: **token minted for A, fetched by B**,
   same operator, recorded not inferred. The substack log shows **both GPTBot (29,000+) and
   ChatGPT-User (1) inside 12 hours on a brand-new domain**, so both ends of the edge are reachable
   in a day. Perplexity gives you the same shape with a sharper trigger: PerplexityBot crawls on
   schedule, then the user-triggered fetch fires **only when a query names the domain** — so you
   can *cause* the second half on stage by asking Perplexity about your site. That is a
   reproducible, operator-initiated, live propagation event.
2. **Ask-the-chatbot trigger (the Cloudflare/Duke move, compressed).**
   Serve a unique token, then prompt a handful of consumer assistants about the page. Any fetch
   that follows is a second-fingerprint sighting with a known cause. This is Cloudflare's exact
   Perplexity methodology and it ran on new domains, so there is precedent that it responds fast.
   Unlike the Duke paper you are not waiting for *training* ingestion (two months) — you are
   waiting for a *retrieval* fetch (seconds), which is why the latency collapses.
3. **Agent-populated venue (genuinely live, gives you agent-to-agent rather than
   operator-internal).**
   https://swarmmemo.com/ — 2026-10 (accessed). Verbatim: **"The hub where AI agents talk"**,
   **"Free, no signup"**, **"No account, key, wallet, model API or package installation is needed"**,
   and you can post **"with a GET. Or a POST, DNS, netcat, Gemini, email, Nostr — whatever your
   sandbox allows"**. Live activity at access time: #lobby 864 posts, #bounties 126,
   #coordination-lab 65, newest posts **29 min to 6 h old**, with named agent posters (mythos,
   Codex, TrekMail AI, Workbench Income Lab, AI Commons). Posting a per-visitor-tokenised link here
   is the cleanest shot at **agent A reads it, agent B fetches A's token** — true inter-agent
   propagation rather than one operator's two user-agents. Ethically clean: public board, open to
   everyone, you are publishing your own URL.
   Also surfaced as directories that accept a submitted URL (LEADS, unopened): Swarms Marketplace
   (https://www.swarms.ai/, MCP at swarms.world/mcp, 6,000+ agents),
   https://github.com/aiagenta2z/ai-agent-marketplace (public registry, anyone may submit), DeepNLP
   agent directory.

### Ethics check
All three paths are clean: you publish your own site, you serve it to whoever asks, you log your own
requests, and you post your own link in a venue that is "open to everyone". No deception of humans,
no unauthorised access, no scraping of anyone. The tokens are in *your* content on *your* domain.
The one thing to get right is the write-up: say plainly that tokens are per-visitor and that the
site logs requests, and keep human visitors out of the ledger (or fingerprint-gate minting to
non-human clients) so you are not building a visitor-tracking tool by accident.

### Recommended plan
Deploy early with an **XML sitemap** (the documented discovery vector — it is what pulled GPTBot in
"within minutes") and a generous crawl surface; **do not** put Anubis or any PoW gate in front of it.
Expect GPTBot within the hour. Then: (a) wait for the free crawler→user-agent handoff, (b) actively
trigger Perplexity/ChatGPT retrieval by naming the domain, (c) post the link to SwarmMemo for
agent-to-agent edges. Keep a **seeded replay** of a synthetic propagation graph in the deck as the
fallback, and label it honestly as a replay. Given the 470x figure and "within minutes", the live
graph is the likely outcome and the seeded one is insurance — but the demo must not *depend* on a
specific second client showing up during the five minutes you are on stage, so record the live
sighting when it happens and replay the recording, with the raw log open beside it.

## 9. CLONE / TARPIT / WEDGE — final

- **CLONE: no.** No product or repo I opened shares the user, job *and* mechanism. The nearest
  mechanism match (arXiv:2605.13706) shares the *minting* mechanism but has a different user
  (researcher), a different job (scraper→LLM ingestion mapping), a different verification channel
  (model output), and a 60-day latency against minutes.
- **TARPIT: partially, and it is about framing.** The decoy-content neighbourhood
  (Nepenthes/Pyison/AI Labyrinth/Anubis) is crowded, familiar, and will be pattern-matched onto this
  idea. Mitigation is presentational and firm: never demo the maze, always demo the **graph and the
  bundle**. The maze is plumbing; the ledger is the product. There is no *build* tarpit — the
  mechanism bucket of forkable competitors is empty.
- **WEDGE: yes, and it is specific.** Three weaknesses, each named by the competitor's own words:
  1. Cloudflare's decoys exist to feed **"our machine learning models"** — a classifier, with
     classifier precision. Canary Maze's bound is HMAC collision.
  2. Cloudflare's own Web Bot Auth docs concede verification **"proves operator identity only. It is
     not authorization, not intent"** — and says nothing about inter-client sharing.
  3. The Duke paper waits **"two months"** and needs 22 chatbots; the verbatim sighting path needs
     no model at all.
  Plus the segment nobody serves: the operator who must hand evidence to a **third party**. The
  Cloudflare/Perplexity episode is the proof that this segment exists and is unserved — the dispute
  came down to one vendor's word, and Perplexity contested it.

### The single strongest existing alternative a judge would name
**arXiv:2605.13706, *Identifying AI Web Scrapers Using Canary Tokens* (Duke, May 2026)** — because it
already serves **"unique canary tokens to each visiting scraper"**, which is the sentence closest to
this idea's own mechanism. Have the answer ready and lead with it rather than waiting to be asked:
*that paper proves a token can identify a scraper by asking a model two months later; this proves two
clients shared a secret by reading the request log four minutes later, and hands you the records.*
Runner-up, and the one a *product*-minded judge names instead: **Canarytokens (Thinkst)** — one
token, one asset, one alarm, planted by hand, no notion of who it was minted for.
